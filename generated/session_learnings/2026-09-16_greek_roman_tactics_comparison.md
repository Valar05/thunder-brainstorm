# Greek + Roman Tactical Contrast — Thunder Design Card

Date: 2026-09-16
Status: synthesis, not canon

## Core distinction

**Greek:** collective geometry, cohesion, terrain, concentration, and personal discipline.

**Roman:** modularity, reserves, command hierarchy, engineering, logistics, and recovery from disruption.

## Design contrast

| Axis | Greek | Roman |
|---|---|---|
| Primary strength | Cohesive formation | Recoverable organization |
| Main tactical question | Where and how does the line meet? | What does the army do when the line stops behaving? |
| Formation model | Dense collective body | Hierarchical modules |
| Terrain | Determines usable formation geometry | Determines routes, logistics, and engineering requirements |
| Reserve | Often tied to planned concentration and delayed commitment | Explicit future resource for restoring a broken line |
| Command | Presence, example, local leadership | Layered orders, standards, messengers, delegated command |
| Engineering | Useful but less central to the core infantry identity | Battlefield infrastructure is a major tactical system |
| Valor | Hold, close, carry, rescue, endure | Repair, rally, reinforce, recover, communicate, restore |
| Failure fantasy | The formation fractures | The system absorbs the fracture and rebuilds |

## Unified Thunder insight

The three historical layers now form a useful progression:

**Greek -> preserve the shape.**

**Roman -> preserve the system.**

**Renaissance -> synchronize the clocks.**

A future Thunder game can combine all three without becoming a history museum:

- Greek mechanics provide formation geometry and cohesion.
- Roman mechanics provide reserves, logistics, command layers, and recovery.
- Renaissance mechanics provide explicit combined-arms timing windows.

The individual hero then has three different roles depending on the layer:

- Greek hero: **the body that refuses to open a gap.**
- Roman hero: **the hand that repairs the machine while it is breaking.**
- Renaissance hero: **the person who accepts danger precisely when another weapon or formation needs time.**
