# Roman Tactics + Individual Valor Brainstorm

Date: 2026-09-16
Source type: historical military patterns transformed into generalized game mechanics
Status: brainstorm material, not canon

## Design target

Roman combat should feel like a **system that can keep functioning after something goes wrong**.

The distinctive game logic is not simply “better infantry.” Roman armies across different periods used different organizations and tactics, but recurring patterns include adaptable formations, reserves, command hierarchy, camp construction, roads, logistics, siegecraft, and engineering. The battlefield is supported by a machine that can repair itself, change shape, and keep pressure on an objective.

Individual valor matters because the Roman system creates meaningful jobs for individuals: close a gap, carry an order, rally a cohort, repair a bridge, hold a gate, protect an aquilifer, recover a standard, stabilize a flank, or lead a counterattack before disorder spreads.

## Tactical pattern vocabulary

### Manipular / modular battle line

Middle Republican Roman armies are especially useful for the abstraction of **small formations operating inside a larger plan**. The classic manipular arrangement is associated with flexibility compared with a single continuous phalanx.

**Mechanic:** break the army into modules with local goals, spacing, and command relationships. Each module can advance, hold, rotate, support, or reinforce without requiring the entire line to perform one identical action.

This makes “formation” a hierarchy rather than one giant object.

### Depth through reserves

A reserve is not merely extra health. Its value comes from appearing where the opponent has exhausted itself.

**Mechanic:** every major force has a committed line and a reserve pool. Sending the reserve early gives immediate power but removes the answer to a later rupture.

The player is constantly trading **present strength for future optionality**.

### Relief and replacement

Roman formations could sustain combat through controlled replacement and local rotation more effectively than a brittle single line.

**Mechanic:** when a front unit reaches a fatigue threshold, a nearby reserve can replace it. This reduces immediate combat power but preserves overall effectiveness.

The result is a combat system where staying power is an active decision.

### Cohorts as local tactical engines

For later Roman armies, the cohort is a useful game-scale unit because it can act semi-independently while remaining part of a legion.

**Mechanic:** the player gives broad orders to the legion but issues precise local commands to cohorts that are under unusual pressure.

This creates a two-level command game:

- strategic intent at legion scale
- local improvisation at cohort scale

### Pilum / disruption before contact

Roman javelin use gives the player a pre-contact decision window: harass, disrupt, or conserve ammunition.

**Mechanic:** the first exchange changes enemy formation quality before melee begins. A damaged shield line, disrupted cavalry, or shaken formation enters the melee with a state penalty.

This is not primarily about projectile damage. It is about **changing the quality of contact**.

### Gladius / short-range exploitation

Roman close combat can be modeled around fighting effectively after entering the enemy's formation rather than maximizing long-distance damage.

**Mechanic:** once a formation breach exists, nearby Roman units gain access to high-value close-range actions that are ineffective against intact frontage.

The important transition is **contact -> penetration -> local collapse**.

### Cavalry and auxiliaries as problem solvers

Roman armies routinely depended on non-legionary capabilities for roles that legionaries were not optimized to perform.

**Mechanic:** auxiliaries are not inferior versions of legionaries. Give them specialist functions such as screening, pursuit, reconnaissance, missile support, rough-terrain fighting, or anti-cavalry work.

A player who refuses to use the right specialist for the problem should create avoidable friction.

### Testudo as conditional protection

The testudo is best treated as a specialized formation response, not an always-on super-defense.

**Mechanic:** the formation can sacrifice movement, offensive capability, and turning speed to gain protection from overhead or missile fire.

This creates an explicit conversion:

**mobility + offense -> protection**.

### Castrum / marching camp

One of the strongest Roman patterns is that defensive preparation is systematic rather than improvised.

**Mechanic:** before or during an operation, the army can spend labor to establish a temporary fortified camp. The camp creates a secure anchor for wounded troops, supplies, command, and reserves.

A camp therefore becomes a **strategic save point made from engineering time**.

**Mechanic:** roads reduce movement friction, bridges create strategic choke points, and supply routes determine how long a force can remain effective.

The battlefield should sometimes be won before contact by choosing which road, ford, or bridge is functional.

### Siege engineering

Roman engineers should make the player think of fortifications as problems to be solved rather than walls with hit points.

**Mechanic:** the player chooses among:

- assault
- mining / undermining
- ramps and earthworks
- artillery preparation
- blockade
- diversion
- breaching equipment

Each option changes time, casualties, logistics, and future battlefield state.

## Individual valor patterns

### Aquilifer / standard bearer

The eagle or other standard is not merely decorative. It is a physical anchor for identity and morale.

**Game version:** the standard can be advanced, withdrawn, protected, or deliberately exposed to stabilize a wavering formation.

A unit that loses its standard should not necessarily instantly rout. Instead, it enters a dangerous **identity-loss state** until the standard is recovered or another command anchor replaces it.

### Centurion at the gap

The ideal Roman individual-valor mechanic is the centurion who turns a local disaster into a manageable problem.

**Mechanic:** when a formation loses cohesion, a nearby leader can personally enter the threatened space and temporarily restore order. The leader's survival then becomes part of the tactical equation.

### Signifer / messenger under pressure

A commander cannot control a distributed army without communications.

**Mechanic:** commands have a delivery path. Messengers, standards, horns, runners, and signal posts restore or extend command range.

A brave messenger can create tactical effects without killing anyone.

### Bridge repair under pursuit

A soldier or engineer can remain exposed to restore a damaged crossing while the main force withdraws.

**Mechanic:** the player can assign a named character to a repair task while enemy pressure continues. Each completed repair stage increases the survival probability of the main body, but each stage raises the character's danger.

### Counterattack after a breach

Roman units can be framed as dangerous precisely because they do not have to accept the initiative after a line breaks.

**Mechanic:** a cohort or named commander may launch a short counterattack into an enemy unit that has overextended. The action can restore local geometry but risks the reserve being consumed early.

### Standard recovery

Recovering a lost standard becomes a contained high-risk mission.

**Mechanic:** when a standard falls, the player may dispatch a small team or individual to recover it. Success restores morale and identity; failure compounds disorder.

### Engineer under fire

Engineering personnel should not be passive support units.

**Mechanic:** building, breaching, repairing, and bridging are active battlefield actions. A character can choose to finish the job under fire rather than retreat.

The resulting value is measurable in turns, routes, and retained capabilities.

### Medic / wounded evacuation decision

Roman organizations support the idea that preserving experienced personnel has long-term value.

**Game version:** wounded characters can be evacuated, carried, left under guard, or returned to a fortified camp. The choice affects the current line and future roster quality.

### Commander sharing danger

A commander can be physically close to threatened troops, improving local response but risking command disruption if lost.

**Mechanic:** command has both **range** and **exposure**. A general who stays safe preserves strategic control; a commander who moves forward can rescue a local situation at greater personal risk.

## Roman encounter recipes

### The line breaks, but the army does not

The key Roman scenario is not preventing every failure. It is demonstrating recovery.

**Sequence:**

1. Front unit takes heavy pressure.
2. A gap opens.
3. A reserve moves into the gap.
4. A centurion restores local cohesion.
5. A second unit rotates out to recover.
6. Cavalry or auxiliaries exploit the enemy's temporary exposure.
7. The line advances again.

The player should feel that **losing a local exchange is survivable when the system remains intact**.

### River crossing

The army must move across a bridge or ford while maintaining command and preventing pursuit.

**Tactical variables:** bridge width, engineer time, reserve location, rearguard, supply wagons, and enemy pressure.

**Valor hooks:** repair the bridge, carry an order, rescue an officer, hold the far bank, or recover a dropped standard.

### Forced march

The army must reach a location before an enemy can prepare.

**Mechanic:** marching faster increases fatigue and reduces cohesion on contact. Infrastructure lowers the cost of speed.

This makes roads mechanically valuable rather than decorative.

### Fortified camp defense

The player uses a camp as the stable center of a mobile army.

**Mechanic:** the camp protects supplies and reserves while forward cohorts fight outside it. The player may fall back deliberately rather than treat retreat as failure.

### Siege with engineering race

The player is attacking a fortified position while defenders attempt countermeasures.

**Mechanic:** each engineering project has a visible progress state, can be interrupted, and can change the available assault routes.

The interesting resource is often **days survived**, not raw damage.

## Individual valor scoring

Valor should be measured by operational consequence:

- restores a broken command link
- closes a formation gap
- preserves a standard
- buys time for a reserve to arrive
- repairs a critical bridge
- rescues an experienced leader
- stabilizes a retreat
- completes an engineering objective under pressure
- turns an enemy overextension into a counterattack

A soldier who saves the army's structure is more important to the system than a soldier with a spectacular kill count.

## Three Roman clocks

1. **Formation clock:** how quickly local order degrades.
2. **Reserve clock:** when the player must spend future options to repair the present.
3. **Infrastructure clock:** whether roads, bridges, camps, supplies, and engineering projects remain available long enough to support the army.

Individual valor becomes the act of **spending personal risk to preserve the system while another clock catches up**.

## Prototype-sized version

One legion-scale battlefield represented by six cohorts, one reserve, one cavalry/auxiliary unit, one camp, one bridge, and four named characters.

The prototype succeeds when it demonstrates:

- modular formations rather than one rigid line
- a reserve that matters because it can be spent
- a breach that can be repaired rather than simply “healed”
- specialists solving problems legionaries cannot solve efficiently
- engineering changing the battlefield
- command range and communication as physical properties
- individual valor preserving the larger system

## Strong Roman synthesis

The Roman tactical fantasy is **resilience through organization**.

Greek material asks: “Can the formation hold?”

Renaissance material asks: “Can different arms synchronize their moments?”

Roman material asks: **“What happens when the plan fails, and can the machine repair itself while still fighting?”**

That makes the Roman layer especially useful for Thunder because it turns failure itself into a tactical resource: the player learns to recover, reinforce, rotate, communicate, engineer, and continue.
